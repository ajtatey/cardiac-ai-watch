## Editor's note
This is the first brief, so it covers roughly the last four months of FDA activity and the last week of everything else. Three things stand out.

**Elucid now has plaque and physiology.** FDA cleared Elucid FFR-IQ on 29 September under the PJA code with HeartFlow FFRct as predicate, eleven days after a refreshed PlaqueIQ clearance. Elucid is the first company other than HeartFlow with a cleared CT-derived FFR product in the US, and the only one with both a plaque and an FFR product under one roof. Expect them to pursue the FFR-CT Category I codes alongside plaque analysis.

**Caristo created a new regulatory category.** The CaRi-Heart De Novo granted on 28 July established product code SIU, "Predictive Indicator For Long-Term Cardiovascular Outcomes," under a new regulation 870.2215. That is the first US authorization of a CCTA-derived long-term risk prediction claim. It also opens a 510(k) path for anyone, including Cleerly, who wants a prognostic claim rather than a measurement claim. The decision summary is not yet posted; the system will keep retrying.

**Scanner vendors are moving into coronary analysis.** Siemens cleared syngo.CT Coronary Cockpit in June, an automated coronary CT analysis and reporting package that ships with the console, and this week announced it is exiting general ultrasound to concentrate on cardiovascular care. Console-bundled analysis at near-zero marginal cost is a different kind of competitor from a standalone AI vendor.

Also worth a look: a JCCT paper quantifying fifteen years of Medicare payment erosion for cardiac CT, and a Budoff-group firefighter cohort using AI-assisted quantitative plaque analysis, which may be Cleerly data. The HeartFlow CEO's first post-IPO share sales were routine plan sales.

  _Primary evidence behind the companion review; the Chinese AI coronary ecosystem (Shukun, Keya) is a longer-term competitive threat._  
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42859700/ (pubmed, 2026-09-08)
- **[P2] Quantification of plaque and detection of high-risk plaque characteristics based on coronary computed tomography angiography: comparison between superresolution deep learning reconstruction and hybrid iterative reconstruction.**  
  Study comparing super-resolution deep learning reconstruction with hybrid iterative reconstruction for CCTA plaque quantification and high-risk plaque detection (QIMS).  
  _Reconstruction changes shift plaque volumes; AI plaque vendors need to know how SR-DLR affects their measurements and calibration._  
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42861037/ (pubmed, 2026-09-11)
- **[P2] Improvements in the Diagnostic Performance of Coronary Computed Tomography Angiography in the Assessment of Heavily Calcified Plaques Using a Novel Deep Learning Model.**  
  Review article (Sun Z, Reviews in Cardiovascular Medicine) on a deep learning model improving CCTA diagnostic performance in heavily calcified plaques.  
  _Calcium blooming is the main failure mode of CCTA stenosis reads; any deep-learning fix changes the competitive baseline for automated analysis._  
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42859515/ (pubmed, 2026-09-14)
- **[P2] Evaluation of different artificial intelligence-based coronary artery calcium scoring algorithms in nongated chest computed tomography.**  
  Comparison of two AI coronary calcium scoring systems on non-gated chest CT and their relation to stenosis degree (J Res Med Sci).  
  _Opportunistic CAC on non-gated CT is a growing funnel into downstream CCTA and plaque analysis._  
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42872262/ (pubmed, 2026-09-23)
- **[P2] Incremental diagnostic value of coronary CT angiography-derived pericoronary adipose tissue radiomics for identifying hemodynamically significant coronary stenosis.**  
  Retrospective study (screened 21,438 patients) showing CCTA-derived pericoronary adipose tissue radiomics adds diagnostic value for hemodynamically significant stenosis (Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine).  
  _Supports the pericoronary inflammation thesis that Caristo's CaRi-Heart is built on._  
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42871045/ (pubmed, 2026-09-29)
