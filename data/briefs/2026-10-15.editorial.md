This is the first brief, so it covers roughly the last four months of FDA activity and the last week of everything else. Three things stand out.

**Elucid now has plaque and physiology.** FDA cleared Elucid FFR-IQ on 29 September under the PJA code with HeartFlow FFRct as predicate, eleven days after a refreshed PlaqueIQ clearance. Elucid is the first company other than HeartFlow with a cleared CT-derived FFR product in the US, and the only one with both a plaque and an FFR product under one roof. Expect them to pursue the FFR-CT Category I codes alongside plaque analysis.

**Caristo created a new regulatory category.** The CaRi-Heart De Novo granted on 28 July established product code SIU, "Predictive Indicator For Long-Term Cardiovascular Outcomes," under a new regulation 870.2215. That is the first US authorization of a CCTA-derived long-term risk prediction claim. It also opens a 510(k) path for anyone, including Cleerly, who wants a prognostic claim rather than a measurement claim. The decision summary is not yet posted; the system will keep retrying.

**Scanner vendors are moving into coronary analysis.** Siemens cleared syngo.CT Coronary Cockpit in June, an automated coronary CT analysis and reporting package that ships with the console, and this week announced it is exiting general ultrasound to concentrate on cardiovascular care. Console-bundled analysis at near-zero marginal cost is a different kind of competitor from a standalone AI vendor.

Also worth a look: a JCCT paper quantifying fifteen years of Medicare payment erosion for cardiac CT, and a Budoff-group firefighter cohort using AI-assisted quantitative plaque analysis, which may be Cleerly data. The HeartFlow CEO's first post-IPO share sales were routine plan sales.
