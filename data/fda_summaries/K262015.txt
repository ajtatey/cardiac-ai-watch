Intended Use 
Elucid FFR -IQ is a coronary physiological simulation software for the clinical quantitative and 
qualitative analysis of previously acquired Computed Tomography DICOM data for clinically stable 
patients with known or suspected coronary artery disease. It provides FFR -CT (CT-derived FFR) 
values estimated from coronary CTA images using AI -enabled algorithms.  FFR -IQ is intended to 
support the functional evaluation of coronary artery disease.   
The FFR -IQ results are provided to support qualified clinicians to aid in the evaluation and 
assessment of coronary arteries. FFR -IQ results are intended to be used by qualified clinicians in 
conjunction with the patient’s clinical history, symptoms, and other diagnostic tests, as well as the 
clinician’s professional judgment. 
Device Description 
FFR-IQ is a BioIntegrated coronary physiological software for the clinical quantitative and 
qualitative analysis of previously acquired Computed Tomography DICOM data for clinically stable 
patients with known or suspected coronary artery disease. It integrates anatomy and biology (such 
as plaque composition and amount which impacts vasodilation) to estimate FFR-CT values from 
static coronary CTA images with extracted coronary tree structures using deep learning neural 
networks. Elucid’s FFR-IQ analysis is intended to support the functional evaluation of Coronary 
Artery Disease. Using the 3D segmentation results of Plaque-IQ, an AI-enabled algorithm assesses 
FFR-CT (CT-derived FFR) values along the coronary tree.  
Elucid’s FFR-IQ is deployed and controlled by Elucid.  FFR-IQ is AI-enabled.

Summary of Clinical Validation 
Study Design 
ELUCIDATE (NCT06018194) is a retrospective, multicenter study that included eleven study sites in 
the US (9) and OUS (2), representative of a generalized population and real-world use conditions. 
Elucid designed this retrospective study to reflect expected commercial performance, rather than 
the performance of a prospective study where CT image acquisition and invasive FFR data 
collection protocols are rigorously supervised, controlled, and unlikely to represent real-world 
conditions.   
The retrospective study design required paired CCTA and invasive FFR for validation purposes; 
however, the population receiving invasive FFR is necessarily narrower than the population 
receiving FFR-CT due to the referral pathway that produces both diagnostic tests on the same 
patient. Under AHA/ACC guidance (Lawton et al. 2021) invasive FFR is performed only when non-
invasive assessment cannot resolve the clinical question, predominantly intermediate 40-69% 
diameter stenosis. The retrospective validation cohort requiring paired CCTA and invasive FFR is 
therefore drawn from that referred subset and is enriched for borderline disease and significantly 
weighted in the FFR gray zone of 0.75 - 0.85.  The US dataset reflects this enriched population; 
therefore, the US dataset and subsequent performance reflect a more challenging intended use 
population than the broader population expected commercially. 
Elucid enrolled data from an OUS site which previously collected the data prospectively to 
represent the portion of the population that is deferred from further invasive assessment. The 
prospective study protocol under which the data was originally acquired encouraged invasive FFR 
measurement in all territories regardless of disease state. Thus, clearly non-obstructive, non-
ischemic vessels are supplemented where retrospective data is sparse. Differences in the 
prospective data collection (as defined by the collection protocol for that study) drive differences in 
the disease prevalence between the US and OUS sites, as well as between the retrospectively and 
prospectively collected data.  To this end, the performance results are reported for the US cohort, 
the OUS cohort, and the overall validation cohort respectively. 
Subjects ages 18 and over with clinically indicated CCTA and invasive FFR performed within 60 days 
of CCTA.  Study design ensured blinding of FFR-CT processing to invasive FFR.  The study analyzed 
491 vessels across 347 patients. 
Enrollment exclusion criteria for patients included: 
• Prior coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) or percutaneous intervention (PCI)
• History of myocardial infarction prior to CCTA and/or invasive FFR
• Known complex congenital heart disease
• Existing pacemaker or internal defibrillator lead implantation
• Existing prosthetic heart valve
• Interval clinical myocardial infarction, diagnosis of decompensated heart failure, stroke, or
coronary revascularization between CCTA and invasive FFR

FFR-IQ was used to simulate FFR-CT from the subject’s CT. The FFR-CT was then compared to the 
invasively collected FFR to assess the device performance. 
Primary Safety and Effectiveness Endpoints 
The primary endpoints regarding this study were sensitivity and specificity to have the lower limits 
of their 95% CI to be > 75%. These performance goals were not necessarily determined to be 
sufficient for substantial equivalence, but the performance demonstrated by the results was. 
Study Results 
The ELUCIDATE overall study results as well as the separate US cohort met the primary endpoints.

Conclusion 
While there are differences noted in the technological characteristics of the proposed device and

device does not, the differences do not raise different questions of safety or effectiveness.  Non-
clinical verification and validation, along with clinical performance data, collectively support 
substantial equivalence of the subject device to the predicate.
