Device Description Summary 21 CFR 807.92(a)(4) 
Quicktome is a software-only, cloud-deployed, image processing package which can be used to perform DICOM image viewing, image 
processing, and analysis. 
Quicktome can receive ("import") DICOM images from picture archiving and communication systems (PACS), acquired with magnetic 
resonance imaging (MRI) or head computed tomography (CT). Image types including but not limited to Tl, T2, FLAIR, and FGATIR 
images, and 4D sequences, including Diffusion Weighted Imaging (DWI) sequences and Resting State functional MRI (rs-fMRI) blood­
oxygen-level-dependent (BOLD) datasets. Once received, Quicktome removes protected health information (PHI) and links the dataset 
to an encryption key, which is then used to relink the data back to the patient when the data is exported to hospital PACS or other 
DICOM device. 
Quicktome is structured as a configurable base platform with optional Application Modules. The base platform supports DICOM image 
viewing, image manipulation, basic measurement, multi-modality image fusion (including MRI-MRI and MRI-CT fusion), 3D visualization 
(MPR reconstructions and 3D volume rendering), workflow-driven quality checks, and target export creation and selection. The 
Application Modules extend the base platform with workflow-specific processing and visualization tools (e.g., tractography generation). 
This submission module includes the ability to co-register a CT with the patient's MRI, automatically extract the geometry of an 
implanted deep brain stimulation (DBS) lead from the CT, and present the lead model alongside registered deep-brain structures as an 
adjunctive visualization aid. 
The software provides a set of module-based workflows for a clinician to: 
* Select (an) image(s) for planning and visualization, 
* Validate image quality, 
* Explore available anatomical regions (including deep-brain structures), network templates, tractography bundles, and parcels, 
* Lreate, detect, select, and adJust segmentation obJects, including regions ot interest, 
* Fuse multiple imaging modalities for combined visualization (for example, anatomical MRI with secondary MRI sequences, DWI, BOLD, 
or CT), 
* Display resting-state fMRI (BOLD) correlation maps using task-analogous seeds for Motor, Vision, and Language networks, 
* Visualize an implanted DBS lead's position and orientation in a CT and against the patient MR data and processing, and 
* Export black-and-white and color DICOMs for use in systems that can view DICOM images. 
Selected processing steps - including skull stripping and atlas registration - are performed by locked machine-learning components. The 
identity, training population, and lifecycle controls applicable to these components are described in an Al/ML Model Card. 
Intended Use/Indications for Use 21 CFR 807.92(a)(5) 
Quicktome Software Suite is composed of a set of modules intended for display of medical images and other healthcare data. It includes 
functions for image review, image manipulation, basic measurements, planning, image fusion, 3D visualization (MPR reconstructions 
and 3D volume rendering) and display of BOLD (blood oxygen level dependent) resting-state MRI scan studies. 
Modules are available for image processing, atlas-assisted visualization and segmentation, resting state analysis and visualization, 
automated and manual segmentation object creation, detection, and adjustment, and target export creation and selection, where an 
output can be generated for use by a system capable of reading DICOM image sets. 
Quicktome is indicated for use in the processing of diffusion-weighted MRI sequences into 3D maps that represent white-matter tracts 
based on constrained spherical deconvolution methods and for the use of said maps to select and create exports. Quicktome can 
generate motor, language, and vision resting state fMRI correlation maps using task-analogous seeds. Quicktome can be used in 
conjunction with other clinical methods as an aid in visualization of deep brain structures, such as the subthalamic nuclei (STN), globus 
pallid us interna (GPi) and ventral is intermediate nucleus (VIM). 
Typical users of Quicktome are medical professionals, including but not limited to surgeons, clinicians, and radiologists. 
Indications for Use Comparison 21 CFR 807.92(a)(5) 
The subject device and the primary predicate share the same intended use: both are software-only, adjunctive image-processing, image­
fusion, and visualization tools that aid medical professionals in the visualization and review of anatomical structures in neurological 
procedures. Both devices are limited to providing adjunctive information to the clinician; neither performs diagnosis, actively guides 
surgical lead placement, performs DBS programming, or models volume of tissue activated. 
Two differences in scope exist between the subject and predicate. Each is a reduction in scope relative to the predicate: 
1. Workflow scope. The primary predicate is indicated for pre-operative planning, intraoperative functional planning (with 3D output 
usable by stereotactic image-guided surgery or other devices), and post-operative review. The subject device is limited to adjunctive 
visualization and post-operative review of pre-operative MRI co-registered to post-operative CT. 
2. Deep brain anatomical coverage. The primary predicate's atlas-assisted segmentation covers STN, GPe, GPi, and VIM. The subject 
device covers STN, GPi, and VIM only; GPe is not included and no claim regarding GPe is made. 
The differences are not critical to the intended use of the device, and they do not affect its safety and effectiveness when used as 
labeled. The device is adjunctive and non-therapeutic; it does not itself diagnose, treat, or guide a surgical procedure, and its outputs are 
returned to PACS in DICOM format for clinician review with no direct interface to surgical-navigation or DBS-programming systems. The 
device is fully verified and validated for the targets and capabilities it does claim, therefore, these differences in Indications for Use do 
not raise new or different questions of safety or effectiveness. 
Technological Comparison 21 CFR 807.92(a)(6) 
The subject device (Quicktome Software Suite) shares the same fundamental technological characteristics as the primary predicate 
device (SIS System, K241083). Both are software-only, Class II (21 CFR 892.2050) medical devices cleared under product codes QIH and 
LLZ, operate on off-the-shelf hardware, are DICOM-compliant, and provide only adjunctive information to clinicians. Neither device 
applies energy to the patient or has any physical, electrical, mechanical, chemical, thermal, or radiation output, and biocompatibility and 
sterility are not applicable to either. 
In terms of core functionality, technology, and performance, both the subject device and the primary predicate: 
1. Allow import and export of DICOM images to and from a hospital PACS; 
2. Provide a graphical user interface for the visualization and review of medical images; 
3. Ingest pre-operative MRI and post-operative CT and perform automated image fusion (co-registration) using rigid and deformable 
registration methodologies; 
4. Produce patient-specific anatomical overlays of deep brain structures, including STN, GPi, and VIM; 
5. Automatically detect implanted DBS leads in post-operative CT and provide manual tools for adjustment of the lead model; 
6. Are software-only, hardware-agnostic solutions that require no proprietary local installation or specialized hardware to perform their 
intended medical function; 
7. KequIre user authentIcatIon and logon; 
8. Provide adjunctive information only- neither device performs surgical guidance, DBS programming, or VT A modeling. 
The technological characteristics carried over from the predecessor Quicktome Software Suite clearance (K222359) are unchanged in the 
subject device. These include DWI-based constrained-spherical-deconvolution (CSD) tractography, resting-state fMRI correlation 
mapping for motor, language, and vision networks, cortical atlas-assisted visualization using a modified Glasser/HCP-MMP1 atlas, 
network templates and tractography bundles, target export generation, the three-tier cloud architecture (browser-based Web Viewer, 
on-premises Gateway for PACS integration and de-identification, and a cloud Processing Core), and two-factor user authentication. 
These features are configurable per facility and were not modified for this submission; their equivalence was confirmed by comparison 
testing against Quicktome (K222359). 
Two technological differences are identified between the subject device and the primary predicate. Neither raises new or different 
questions of safety or effectiveness, for the reasons summarized below; each was verified and validated and did not lead to any 
additional questions of safety or effectiveness. 
Difference 1: Segmentation algorithm 
The predicate generates anatomical overlays using pre-trained deep-learning neural network models. The subject device generates 
equivalent patient-specific overlays by deformably registering a locked, Omniscient-built atlas into each patient's MRI space. Both 
approaches deliver the same output to the clinician: a patient-specific 3D anatomical overlay derived from a clinical MR acquisition, 
displayed adjunctively, using a population-derived reference and producing locked, deterministic outputs at deployment. Algorithmic 
verification against an independent atlas-derived reference standard (the AHEAD 7T atlas, precedented by reference device K253379) 
demonstrated STN and GPi pass at acceptance criteria consistent with the primary predicate's 2.0 mm threshold. Clinician-based clinical 
validation by three board-certified DBS-experienced neurosurgeons demonstrated STN, GPi, and VIM are acceptable using pre-defined 
acceptance criteria. 
Difference 2: Automated lead roll/ yaw (rotational orientation) detection 
The predicate provides automated rotational-orientation (roll/ yaw) detection of directional DBS leads from post-operative CT. The 
subject device performs automated lead segmentation and localization (position, length, diameter, and lead positions) but does not 
output an automated estimate of rotational orientation. The summative evaluation conducted per IEC 62366-1 and FDA Human Factors 
guidance with representative DBS-experienced neurosurgeons, passed with no critical use errors or use-related hazards traceable to the 
absence of this feature, confirming that the intended adjunctive post-operative review workflow is performed acceptably without it. 
Comparison to the primary predicate device, in conjunction with the design verification and validation activities described in this 
submission, supports the substantial equivalence of the Quicktome Software Suite. The device performs as intended under the specified 
conditions of use and does not raise new or different questions of safety or effectiveness when compared to the legally marketed 
predicate device. 
Non-Clinical and/or Clinical Tests Summary & Conclusions 21 CFR 807.92(b) 
Substantial equivalence is supported by nonclinical software verification and validation testing, including bench performance testing 
against independent reference standards and clinician-based clinical validation of the device's outputs. 
The following FDA-recognized consensus standards were applied: 
• IEC 62304 - Medical device software - Software life cycle processes; 
• IEC 62366-1 - Application of usability engineering to medical devices; 
• ISO 14971 - Application of risk management to medical devices; 
• NEMA PS 3.1-3.20- Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine (DICOM); 
• TIR97 - Principles for medical device security- Postmarket risk management for device manufacturers; 
• SW96 - Standard for medical device security- Security risk management for device manufacturers; 
• TIR57 - Principles for medical device security- Risk management. 
Comparative performance testing followed the methodology used by the primary predicate (K241083) where applicable. Established 
reference methodologies were used where appropriate: the AHEAD ultra-high-field 7T atlas, an approach precedented by reference 
device K253379, was used as an independent reference standard for STN and GPi segmentation testing.

• Skull stripping verification. 
• Cortical anatomical region and atlas generation. Comparison to the previously cleared Quicktome Software Suite (K222359) confirmed 
equivalence of the generated cortical anatomical regions. 
• Tractography bundle generation. Comparison to the previously cleared Quicktome Software Suite (K222359) confirmed equivalence of 
the generated tractography bundles. 
• Deep structure segmentation (STN, GPi). A 7TT1 atlas was used to establish the segmentation reference standard. Prespecified 
acceptance criteria (~ 90% of center-of-mass and mean symmetric surface distances~ 2.0 mm, consistent with the threshold cleared for

• CT-MRI co-registration. Acceptance criteria were aligned with the primary predicate methodology. 
• CT lead extraction (lead segmentation and localization). Positional-error acceptance criteria (tip and centerline~ 1.5 mm) were 
modeled on the predicate (K241083). 
• Clinical validation of deep structure segmentation (STN, GPi, VIM). Algorithm-derived overlays were rated against the patient's

• Design validation and summative usability evaluation, conducted in accordance with IEC 62366-1 and FDA Human Factors guidance 
with representative end users (DBS-experienced neurosurgeons). 
All verification and validation activities required by the verification and validation plan were performed using a production-equivalent 
deployment of the software. Each identified technological difference between the subject device and the primary predicate was verified 
and validated and did not lead to any additional questions of safety or effectiveness. Risk management activities concluded that the 
benefits of the device outweigh the residual risks. 
No clinical tests were submitted to support this submission. 
Based on the comparison of intended use and technological characteristics, the nonclinical testing summarized above, and the risk­
management conclusions, Omniscient Neurotechnology concludes that the Quicktome Software Suite is substantially equivalent to the 
predicate device, the SIS System (K241083). 
The subject device has the same intended use as the primary predicate - image processing, image fusion, visualization, and adjunctive 
review of deep brain structures (STN, GPi, VIM) in neurological procedures. The subject device and the predicate share the same product 
codes (QIH, LLZ), the same regulation (21 CFR 892.2050), the same Class II classification, the same software-only modality on off-the-shelf 
hardware, the same DICOM compatibility, the same adjunctive (non-therapeutic, non-navigational) role, and the same general 
algorithmic approach to MRI-CT fusion, deep-structure overlay generation, and DBS lead identification from post-operative CT. 
The bench performance testing demonstrated that the device's segmentation, co-registration, and lead-extraction outputs meet 
prespecified acceptance criteria consistent with those cleared for the predicate, evaluated against independent reference standards. The 
clinician-based clinical validation demonstrated that the device's anatomical overlays are rated as adequate for the intended use by 
board-certified, DBS-experienced neurosurgeons. The summative usability evaluation confirmed that representative users perform the 
intended adjunctive post-operative review workflow safely and effectively. 
Taken together, the design verification and validation conducted support the conclusion that the Quicktome Software Suite performs as 
intended under the specified conditions of use, and that it is at least as safe and as effective as, and performs as well as, the legally 
marketed predicate device. The device does not raise new or different questions of safety or effectiveness when compared to the 
predicate device and is therefore substantially equivalent.
